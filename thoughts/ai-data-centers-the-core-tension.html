AI Data Centers: The Core Tension

The debate over data‑center expansion keeps surfacing in my feed, and for good reason. Many of us sit in a strange tension: our organizations rely on data centers for affordable cloud compute and storage, and some communities benefit from the tax revenue. Yet the tradeoffs are real: massive power demand, heavy water usage for cooling, noise, higher residential electricity rates, and long‑term environmental impact.

What struck me about this recent piece by Deborah Mayo is her proposal to break out of the usual cycle of hearings, lobbying, and outrage. She suggests adversarial collaboration: a structured process where opposing sides jointly design tests that could genuinely prove either of them wrong.

Imagine shifting the conversation from rhetoric to measurable hypotheses:
• How much grid stress would new facilities actually create?
• What is the real blackout risk under peak load?
• How would pricing change for ratepayers?
• Which mitigation strategies actually work, and which only sound good?

Whether you’re for or against expansion, this approach forces a rare kind of clarity:
What evidence would make us reconsider our position?

That question sits at the heart of critical thinking. In my book, I argue that humility, paired with better tools for evaluating claims is what turns debate into inquiry. Mayo’s piece is a reminder that scientific thinking isn’t just for labs; it’s a practical method for improving public decision‑making.

If more of our hardest policy disputes adopted this mindset, we’d get fewer stalemates and more truth.
